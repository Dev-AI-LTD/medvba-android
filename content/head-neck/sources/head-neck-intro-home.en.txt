Which of the following bones does NOT belong to the neurocranium?
A) Frontal bone
B) Ethmoid bone
C) Zygomatic bone
D) Sphenoid bone
E) Occipital bone
Correct answer: C
Explanation: The neurocranium is composed of 8 bones (frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, occipital, 2 parietals, 2 temporals) which enclose the brain. The zygomatic bone is part of the viscerocranium (splanchnocranium), together with the maxilla, mandible, palatines, lacrimals, nasals, vomer and inferior nasal conchae.
Question 2 — Level: Easy
How many paired bones belong to the neurocranium?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
E) 5
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The neurocranium contains 2 paired bones (parietal and temporal) and 4 unpaired bones (frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, occipital). The other options do not correspond to the classical anatomical organization of the cranium.
Question 3 — Level: Easy
The crista galli is an anatomical landmark located on the:
A) Frontal bone
B) Sphenoid bone
C) Ethmoid bone
D) Occipital bone
E) Petrous part of the temporal bone
Correct answer: C
Explanation: The crista galli is a median bony prominence on the cribriform plate of the ethmoid, in the anterior cranial fossa, serving as attachment for the falx cerebri. It is also the crossing point of the ethmoido-frontal pillar (buttress).
Question 4 — Level: Medium
The sella turcica, housing the pituitary gland, is located on the:
A) Endocranial surface of the frontal bone
B) Body of the sphenoid bone
C) Basilar plate of the occipital
D) Anterior surface of the petrous temporal
E) Cribriform plate of the ethmoid
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The sella turcica is a depression on the superior surface of the sphenoid body, bounded anteriorly by the tuberculum sellae and posteriorly by the dorsum sellae. The pituitary gland sits in the hypophyseal fossa within it.
Question 5 — Level: Easy
Which of the following structures passes through the foramen rotundum?
A) Ophthalmic nerve (V1)
B) Maxillary nerve (V2)
C) Mandibular nerve (V3)
D) Middle meningeal artery
E) Oculomotor nerve
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The maxillary nerve (V2) exits the endocranium through the foramen rotundum, located in the greater wing of the sphenoid, entering the pterygopalatine fossa. The ophthalmic passes through the superior orbital fissure, the mandibular through the foramen ovale, and the middle meningeal artery through the foramen spinosum.
Question 6 — Level: Easy
The mandibular nerve (V3) exits the endocranium through the:
A) Foramen rotundum
B) Superior orbital fissure
C) Foramen ovale
D) Foramen spinosum
E) Foramen of Vesalius
Correct answer: C
Explanation: The mandibular nerve, the largest terminal branch of the trigeminal, traverses the cranial base through the foramen ovale, in the greater wing of the sphenoid. Here it is accompanied by the accessory meningeal artery and reaches the infratemporal fossa.
Question 7 — Level: Medium
The middle meningeal artery, the main branch of the maxillary artery, enters the endocranium through the:
A) Foramen ovale
B) Foramen rotundum
C) Foramen spinosum
D) Carotid canal
E) Hiatus of Fallopius
Correct answer: C
Explanation: The middle meningeal artery is the intracranial branch of the maxillary artery and enters through the foramen spinosum in the greater wing of the sphenoid. Its injury (frequent in pterion fractures) causes acute epidural hematoma, a neurosurgical emergency.
Question 8 — Level: Medium

Question 9 — Level: Easy

A) Optic canal exclusively

Explanation: The annulus of Zinn is a common tendinous ring for the four rectus muscles of the eyeball, located at the medial portion of the superior orbital fissure and surrounding the optic canal. Through it pass III, VI, the nasociliary nerve and the ophthalmic artery.
Question 10 — Level: Hard
The symptomatic triad of cavernous sinus syndrome includes lesion of the following nerves EXCEPT:
A) Oculomotor nerve (III)
B) Trochlear nerve (IV)
C) Ophthalmic nerve (V1)
D) Abducens nerve (VI)

Explanation: The cavernous sinus contains in its lateral wall nerves III, IV, V1, V2 (partial), and within its lumen the internal carotid artery and nerve VI. Question 11 — Level: Easy
The mandible articulates with the temporal bone at the:
A) Lambdoid suture
B) Temporomandibular joint
C) Sphenotemporal suture
D) Squamosal suture
E) Occipito-mandibular joint
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The temporomandibular joint is a bicondylar synovial joint (with articular disc) between the mandibular condyle and the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone. It is the only movable joint of the cranium.
Question 12 — Level: Medium
The articular disc of the temporomandibular joint divides the joint cavity into:
A) A single unified compartment
B) Two compartments: superior (meniscotemporal) and inferior (meniscomandibular)
C) Three equal compartments
D) Two vertical compartments (medial and lateral)
E) Four quadrants
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The fibrous articular disc separates the joint into the supradiscal (meniscotemporal) compartment, where translational movements occur, and the infradiscal (meniscomandibular) compartment, where rotational movements of the condyle take place. This organization allows complex masticatory movements.
Question 13 — Level: Hard
Anterior dislocation of the temporomandibular joint most frequently occurs through:
A) Posterior displacement of the condyle past the articular tubercle
B) Anterior displacement of the condyle past the articular tubercle
C) Fracture of the mandibular neck
D) Rupture of the sphenomandibular ligament

Explanation: Anterior dislocation (most frequent, e.g. during wide mouth opening or yawning) consists of the mandibular condyle sliding anteriorly past the articular tubercle of the temporal bone, becoming locked in the pterygoid fossa. Question 14 — Level: Medium
The four main masticatory muscles receive motor innervation from the:
A) Facial nerve (VII)
B) Mandibular branch of the trigeminal (V3)
C) Glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)

Correct answer: B
Explanation: Masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid and lateral pterygoid receive motor innervation from the motor root of the trigeminal, which joins the V3 (mandibular) branch. These muscles derive embryologically from the first pharyngeal arch, accounting for trigeminal innervation.
Question 15 — Level: Easy
The masseter muscle has as its main action:
A) Depression of the mandible
B) Elevation of the mandible (mouth closure)
C) Mandibular protrusion
D) Lateralization of the tongue
E) Elevation of the soft palate
Correct answer: B
Explanation: The masseter is the main mandibular elevator, together with the temporalis and medial pterygoid. Its insertion runs between the zygomatic arch and the lateral surface of the mandibular ramus. Mandibular depression is performed by the suprahyoid muscles and the lateral pterygoid.
Question 16 — Level: Medium
Which masticatory muscle is mainly responsible for mandibular protrusion?
A) Masseter
B) Temporalis (anterior fibers)
C) Temporalis (posterior fibers)
D) Lateral pterygoid (bilateral action)
E) Medial pterygoid (unilateral action)
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Bilateral contraction of the lateral pterygoid pulls the mandibular condyle and articular disc anteriorly, producing mandibular protrusion. Posterior fibers of the temporalis produce retraction, and unilateral contraction of the lateral pterygoids produces side-to-side (lateral excursion) movements.
Question 17 — Level: Easy
The muscles of facial expression (mimic muscles) are innervated by the:
A) Trigeminal nerve (V)
B) Facial nerve (VII)
C) Glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)
D) Vagus nerve (X)
E) Accessory nerve (XI)
Correct answer: B
Explanation: All muscles of facial expression derive from the second pharyngeal arch and receive motor innervation exclusively from the facial nerve (VII). Facial palsy (Bell) produces ipsilateral facial asymmetry, with inability to close the eye, flattening of the nasolabial fold and drooping of the oral commissure.
Question 18 — Level: Medium

D) Accessory lacrimal function

Correct answer: B
Explanation: The orbicularis oculi closes the palpebral fissure (blinking and forced closure) and is innervated by branches of the facial nerve. Eyelid opening is performed by the levator palpebrae superioris (innervated by III) and by Müller's muscle (sympathetic).
Question 19 — Level: Hard

Question 20 — Level: Easy

Question 49 — Level: Easy
